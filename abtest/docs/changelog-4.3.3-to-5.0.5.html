Firmware 4.3.3 → 5.0.5

This report covers the changes customers will notice when they move from the shipping firmware 4.3.3 (cdcff19) to the current release, 5.0.5 (tag v5.0.5, c38572a). The bench results were measured on 5.0.0 (tag v5.0.0, 48f5fb2), the first release of the series, and were not repeated on later releases. During development 5.0.0 was called 4.5.0, and drives report 4.5.0 on builds before 886889d. Each chart is real data from one production P4-42 on the same bench. It compares 4.3.3 running its shipping CSV (v1.3.3) with the released 5.0.0 running the updated gains (v1.7.0, migrated to 5.0.0's rated units at boot), or shows one 5.0.0 feature switched off and on. A few experiments were run only on the development build 6c37573; their captions say so. Open issues are listed next to the gains. Five maintenance releases followed from 2026-10-30 to 2026-11-06: 5.0.1 (ca6bee0), 5.0.2 (5aa6337), 5.0.3 (8d75659), 5.0.4 (d93342b) and 5.0.5 (c38572a). Their changes are listed at the end of the changelog, and the ones that need action are under Upgrading. 5.0.4 renumbers several fault codes, so host software that decodes them must be updated with it. Do not run 5.0.2 on STM32 drives. 5.0.5 brings the drive in line with CiA 402 and CiA 301 on stopping, status and loss of the master: a moving motor now brakes on a stop where it used to coast, and losing the master faults an enabled drive by default. See In 5.0.5 before moving a system to it.

On the same P4-42, 5.0.0 (tag v5.0.0) with the v1.7.0 gains beats 4.3.3 with the v1.3.3 CSV almost everywhere. The current loop reaches the commanded current exactly, where 4.3.3 overshoots it by 12–28 %. Test setup

One P4-42 (node 4, BO4523 HFM winding, STM32) with a free rotor and no load, on a 42 V bench supply. The host drove the bus over SocketCAN with a 1 kHz SYNC and recorded TPDOs. For 4.3.3 the drive ran cdcff19 with P4-42-42V-20A-BO4523-HFM-v1.3.3-2026-10-15.csv. For 5.0.0 it ran the release build 48f5fb2 (tag v5.0.0) with the drive's own saved configuration: the gains of P4-42-42V-20A-BO4523-HFM-v1.7.0-2026-10-20.csv, exported from the same drive after commissioning, which 5.0.0 migrated to rated units at boot, plus two later commissioning results: the encoder filter at 0x90 (the CSV has 0x70) and the Coulomb friction feed-forward 0x2381,3 on. The settle integrator 0x2381,4 is off. Before the run the current-sense zero was re-measured and set in RAM. The drive's β zero varies by up to 5.6 ADC counts from boot to boot, and a first pass on a boot about 4 counts from the stored bias showed 2–5 times the once-per-electrical-turn speed wobble below 50 rpm. Both configurations keep ipeak at 28.3 A; 5.0.0 bases per-mille on icont, 5.96 A. The 5.0.0 captures were taken on 2026-10-29. The motor heats up during the high-speed and current tests, and two things change with it: the low-speed wobble grows, and the demagnetisation limit falls below the i²t test current. So the low-speed, cogging, decoupling and i²t captures were taken last, with the motor cooled to 40 °C. The experiments marked 6c37573 (the encoder filter and sub-count tables, and the diagnostics) were run on that development build on 2026-10-19 to 2026-10-21 and were not repeated. Speed ripple is the drive's own estimate (0x606C), read at 1 kHz. Host timestamps are not used for any speed figure. The A/B tests turn one feature off in RAM, run the same sequence, and restore it.

STEP RESPONSECurrent, velocity and position

Several fixes change the step response together. The current loop now runs in 32-bit (single-precision) floating point; the old Q15 path truncated small gains. Resistance and inductance use one consistent unit scale. Measured d/q decoupling (with a swapped Ld/Lq term fixed), back-EMF feed-forward and dead-time compensation remove disturbances the current loop used to fight. The speed loop was rewritten so its integrator respects the torque and current limits. The P4-42 also has a new commissioned tune, with gains designed from measured inertia, friction and Kt and then refined on the drive.

Current step on the d axis, from raw α/β current

A step on the q axis spins up the free rotor, and the growing back-EMF corrupts the measurement. The reported current 0x6078 is also filtered, and the filter differs between the two firmwares. So this test steps the d axis: the encoder offset is shifted 90 electrical degrees for the test, and a 2 A torque command then lands on the d axis and makes no torque. The drive streams the raw α/β phase currents and the electrical angle θe at the control rate. The host rotates them into d/q itself, so neither firmware's display filter is involved.

Command4.3.35.0.0

5.0.0 reaches the command and stays there: 2.00–2.01 A for 2.00 A, with a 10–90 % rise of 2.8–3.1 ms and a 0.05–0.07 A overshoot. 4.3.3 settles at 2.23–2.56 A, 12–28 % above the command, and couples up to 0.96 A into the q axis, against 0.10 A on 5.0.0. Its faster rise (1.4–2.1 ms) comes from aiming at the wrong level. The 4.3.3 samples come over a TPDO every 0.3 ms, since 4.3.3 has no control-rate logger. The 5.0.0 samples come from the on-board logger at the 15 kHz control rate.

Why 5.0.0 no longer settles at 90 % of a q command

Early 5.0.0 builds settled at 86–90 % of a q-axis torque step on a free rotor. The cause was the back-EMF. As the rotor accelerates, ωψ ramps up, and a PI follows a ramp with a constant error, (dE/dt)/Ki. The fix (54b0459) adds ωψ to the q voltage as a feed-forward, scaled from Kt, so the integrator carries only the model error. The velocity loop settles far faster. With the 10 ms average, 5.0.0 settles in 15–16 ms against 83–95 ms. The ripple alone spans the raw ±5 % band, so the settling column uses a 10 ms moving average on both firmwares. The overshoot is 21–29 %, against 68–76 % on 4.3.3. That is a few points above the 16–20 % the same gains gave on the development build. It is not the 0x90 encoder filter: a filter sweep on 5.0.0 gave 20–28 % at 0x70 and 0x80 as well. It is not the rated-unit migration either, which preserved the gain in A/rpm exactly. It is what those gains give with the measured 1.88 ms loop delay (about 48° of phase margin). Scaling them down cuts the overshoot but costs position settling (see the encoder filter section). The 5.0.0 position loop (kp 107, velocity feed-forward 1.0) rises in 13–16 ms instead of 51–54 ms, and is within ±1° in 29–41 ms on three of four steps. The fourth step overshot 1.05° and took 201 ms. On 4.3.3 every step took 108–120 ms.

The last 0.1° is slower on 5.0.0: 816–830 ms against 179–233 ms. Part of the difference is resolution. At rest the 5.0.0 axis dithers by about one encoder count (0.09°), where 4.3.3 sits on one count, because the position loop follows the encoder's own noise. On the development build that noise was 0.62 counts rms at rest at the v1.7.0 filter setting (0x70) and 0.66 counts in a hold. 5.0.0 runs with the 0x90 filter and uses the encoder's angle below one count (stm32 6d34de1), which together bring the hold to 0.30–0.34 counts rms and cut the holding current noise by about a quarter, with the same step response. Both are in this run. The encoder filter section has the measurements.

Most of the slow last 0.1° is friction. The axis stops a few counts short of the target and creeps in over 500–800 ms as the velocity integrator builds up enough torque to break the gearhead's stiction. On the development build it did not change with the encoder filter (0x70 to 0xB0), the velocity feed-forward (0 to 1.25), or the sub-count position, and a lower position gain (pkp 44) settled even later. The velocity feed-forward adds nothing to a single-sample step, which is a one-cycle impulse. On a profiled move it cuts the maximum following error from 22.5° to 2.5°.

5.0.0 has two remedies for the creep (stm32 e1b0a4e, cfaea4d). The first is a Coulomb friction feed-forward, 0x2381,3: it adds a fixed torque in the direction the trajectory is moving and cuts the following error during moves by 30–60 %. It is on in this run (10, from the test drive's NV). The second is a settle integrator gain, 0x2381,4: in CSP and PP, once the trajectory has stopped and the error is within 16 counts, the velocity integrator runs at this gain instead of the normal Ki. It cannot charge during the move, so it does not cause the overshoot a higher Ki does. It defaults to 0 and is off in this run, which is why the 0.1° column is long. When the two were developed on the test P4-42, a settle gain of 4×10−4 with the feed-forward at 10 brought the median time to within 0.1° from 470–620 ms to 60–95 ms across PP, CSP and small steps. Overshoot stayed at 0.09°. In 10 s holds the position and torque noise were the same as with it off, with no limit cycle. Commissioning step 16 (settle Ki) sets it per drive.

TRACKING ESTIMATORVelocity feedback at low and high speed

4.3.3 differenced the encoder count once per millisecond. At 30 rpm on a 4096-count encoder that is 2 counts per sample, so the speed signal jumps between whole counts: 0, 14.6 or 29.3 rpm. The speed loop amplifies that quantisation into current ripple. 5.0.0 estimates speed with a tracking observer, a phase-locked loop on the encoder angle that runs at the control rate. It gives a continuous speed and a delay-compensated angle, and it is the source of 0x606C. The encoder runout correction (next section) is applied to the angle before the observer, so the estimator no longer sees the encoder's once- and twice-per-turn error as speed.

ENCODER RUNOUTCorrecting the magnetic encoder's angle error

What encoder runout is

The magnetic encoder reads a magnet on the rotor shaft. A magnet slightly off-centre or tilted gives an angle error that repeats every revolution, mostly once and twice per turn. On a P4-42 it is 8.6 counts rms of 4096, 94 % of it twice per turn; the 11-count peak is about 7° electrical. 4.3.3 had no correction. The error turns into torque ripple through commutation, into position error, and into false speed ripple that the velocity loop then follows with real torque.

The correction in 5.0.0

0x3027 holds two harmonic bins of the error at rest; the drive subtracts Σ [A_s sin(kθ) + A_c cos(kθ)] / 256 from the raw count.

The encoder chip passes its own error through a second-order tracking filter, so at speed each harmonic arrives scaled and rotated by H(jkω). On the P4-42 at 9,500 rpm that is 0.79 and −57°, so a table measured at rest makes things worse there. Since 4c7751b the drive multiplies each bin by the conjugate of H, updated at 1 kHz from the observer's speed. The twice-per-turn residual then stayed at 0.5–1.0 counts from 6,000 to 9,500 rpm both ways, against 9–12.6 counts raw.

At first the correction reached only commutation and the 0x3012,2 display. Since ea5172b it also feeds position 0x6064 and the tracking observer behind 0x606C. Before, a P4-42 at 1000 rpm read 33.5 rpm of twice-per-turn speed ripple, 14 rpm of it real motion; the digital twin reads 1.0 rpm with the change. An erased 0x3027 (a drive upgraded from 4.3.3) counts as off.

Calibration procedure (p4sysid)

The TUI step runout runs p4sysid.encoder.calibrate_runout:

  • Sweep. With compensation off, a d-axis current of at most i_cal holds the rotor in voltage-angle mode. The field steps 64 times per electrical cycle, 25 ms each, one revolution forward and one back, and the raw position is read at each step. The rotor must travel a revolution each way within 10 %, with under 45° electrical error.
  • Fit. The error (field angle minus encoder angle) is fitted as a Fourier series in the raw angle, with one constant per direction for friction lag. Multiples of the pole-pair count are fitted against the field angle: they are the rotor's own cogging motion, not encoder error.
  • Bin choice. Up to two bins. Rejected: k = pole pairs − 1 (stator field reaching the sensor), pole-pair multiples, k > 42, lines under 4× the spectrum median or 0.5 counts, leakage beside strong cogging lines, and per-model skips (k = 12 on the P4-42 HFM). The table's slope must stay under 0.75.
  • Upload. Bins go to 0x3027,2–7. The filter model goes to 0x3027,8–9 from the encoder type and 0x3013,6: 150 Hz / 0.88 for the MA736 at 0xA0, datasheet values for the MA702, none for the MA782.
  • Retest. The sweep repeats on the compensated position. Each bin must fall to half or under 0.5 counts, or compensation is switched off and the step fails. The TUI then re-checks encoder linearity.

The table stays in RAM until the save step. It is per unit and is not in the CSV.

ENCODER FILTERSensor filter, sub-count position and the rest dither

The P4-42's MA736 encoder has a programmable digital filter. It is register 0x0E, set through 0x3013,6, with the filter setting in bits 7:4. That filter acts inside the sensor, before the angle is cut to 12 bits, so it feeds position, the speed observer and commutation alike. The drive also has a software moving average on velocity, 0x2100,1. That one only smooths a signal the observer already differentiates, and the commissioned CSVs leave it bypassed (0). So the sensor filter is the one worth tuning. These tests looked for the setting that removes the rest dither described under the position step without slowing the loops.

So its designs were soft: at the same 62° target the measured-delay design gives kp 0.0199 and ki 1.42e-4 (36 Hz) against 0.0173 and 1.06e-4 (31 Hz) from the model.

What cogging compensation does

A permanent-magnet motor has a detent torque that depends only on rotor angle: the magnets pull toward the stator teeth, and magnet and winding asymmetries add more. At low speed the velocity loop fights it every revolution. 4.3.3 had no compensation. 5.0.0 adds a q-axis feed-forward current, i_ff = Σ [a_s sin(kθ) + a_c cos(kθ)], evaluated every control cycle at the runout-corrected encoder angle θ.

Calibration procedure (p4sysid)

The TUI step cogging runs p4sysid.cogging.calibrate after the velocity loop is tuned:

  • Set-up. Lines that are not cogging are excluded: k = pole pairs (sense offset) and the 0x3027 encoder orders with their ±2 sidebands. The lead budget is the encoder latency (0x3013,10 + 0x3013,5) + 1/(2π·current bandwidth) + one control cycle. The fade is 0.43 × the current-loop bandwidth.
  • Capture. With the feed-forward off, the motor spins in cyclic synchronous velocity at 30 and 60 rpm, both ways, 8 revolutions each. The logger streams iq and position at 1 kHz. Bad frames are dropped; more than 2 % fails the capture.
  • Fit. Per revolution, load torque d = Kt·iq − J·α is fitted as a Fourier series in the corrected angle. A line becomes a bin only if every run agrees to at least 4 standard errors and it exceeds both 1 mA (10 bin units) and 10−4 ipeak. The four strongest are kept.
  • Refine. The bins are written and the capture repeats with them on. The measured iq includes the feed-forward, so the second fit corrects the first.
  • Lead scan. At 60 rpm the budget is tried ×1.0, 1.4, 1.8, 2.2, extended in 0.4 steps (0.6–3.4×) while the best is at an edge. Lowest angle-locked velocity ripple wins.
  • Verify. Off against on at 30, 60 and 120 rpm. A bin that does not lower its own velocity line by 2σ is dropped. If 30 rpm does not improve by 3σ, the step fails and the previous table is restored.

The result stays in RAM until the save step, and is not in the CSV. On the P4-42 (7 pole pairs, 12 slots) the bins are k14, k12, k28 and k84, with a lead near 1.5 ms and a 100 Hz fade; angle-locked ripple at 30 rpm fell from 28 to 4.3 rpm.

DEAD TIMEDead-time compensation

What dead time is

Each phase is a half-bridge of two transistors, and if both conduct at once the bus is shorted. So at every edge the gate driver turns one off and waits before turning the other on. That wait is the dead time, 0x3001,2: 250 ns on the P4-42, 275 ns on the P4-37 and 420 ns on the P4-16.

During the dead time the current flows through a diode, and the phase sits at whichever rail opposes it. Every PWM period loses a fixed volt-second whose sign follows the phase current. The average phase voltage falls short by about Vdt = teff · fpwm · Vbus. On the P4-42 the measured effective time is 201 ns, so at 60 kHz and 42 V that is about 0.5 V per phase. That is more than the resistive drop of a 2 A current.

How it distorts the current

  • Volt-second loss. The error opposes the current, like extra resistance the loop must supply.
  • 6× electrical ripple. Three phases each change sign twice per electrical cycle, so the error vector steps six times per cycle.
  • Zero-crossing dead zone. Near zero current the PWM ripple smears the sign, so the loss grows through a knee. Light loads suffer most.

At low speed the 6× ripple is well inside the current loop's bandwidth and the PI rejects most of it. As speed rises it approaches the bandwidth and leaks through, and the lost voltage comes out of the headroom near top speed.

The feed-forward in 5.0.0

4.3.3 had no compensation. 5.0.0 adds the lost voltage back to the α/β command, after the inverse Park and before modulation, in each phase current's direction. Three objects set it:

DECOUPLINGPMSM d/q decoupling

In the rotor's d/q frame, a PMSM's two current axes are not independent. Current on one axis induces a voltage on the other that grows with electrical speed ω. The rotor magnet adds a back-EMF on the q axis. Per phase:

  • Vd = R·id + Ld·did/dt − ω·Lq·iq
  • Vq = R·iq + Lq·diq/dt + ω·Ld·id + ω·ψ

The current PIs can reject these terms as disturbances, but only within their bandwidth. Decoupling computes the speed terms from the model and adds them to the PI outputs as a feed-forward. The PIs then see a plain R–L load on each axis. 5.0.0 follows NXP's GMCLIB_DecouplingPMSM. It adds −ωLqiq to the d voltage and +ωLdid to the q voltage, using the measured d/q current every control cycle. Ld comes from 0x3011,6 and Lq from 0x3011,12, both in µH line-to-line.

What was wrong before

4.3.3 used a single inductance for both cross terms, so it ignored the motor's saliency. The P4-42 v1.7.0 CSV gives Ld 98 µH and Lq 188 µH line-to-line, almost a factor of two apart. While Ld and Lq were being split during development, the two terms were briefly assigned the wrong way round: the iq term on the d axis used Ld. e649c03 fixed that before release. That bug never shipped.

A second gap remained until 54b0459. GMCLIB_DecouplingPMSM has only the cross terms, so the q integrator carried the back-EMF ωψ itself. On a free rotor accelerating under a torque step, the back-EMF ramps up. A PI follows a ramp with a constant error, (dE/dt)/Ki. The measured current therefore settled at 86–90 % of the command. The q voltage now adds ωψ as a feed-forward, scaled from Kt, so the integrator carries only the model error. On the P4-37 (1 A step, 24 V) the plateau rose from 86–90 % to 99–103 %, and the 500 rpm velocity step's rise time fell from 26 to 22 ms. Coasting was unchanged.

For scale on the P4-42 at 10,000 rpm (ω ≈ 7,330 rad/s electrical, 7 pole pairs): the back-EMF is about 15 V peak per phase, and ωLqiq at 10 A is about 7 V. At 42 V the linear voltage limit is 24 V per phase. Computed from the v1.7.0 CSV values; not measured.

It is RAM-only and is read when the drive is enabled. 0x3016,10 scales the Lq term in per cent (0 means no change). Both are for testing; leave them at 0 in service.

ENCODER LAGWhy the angle lags, and how the trim is calibrated

The drive commutates on an encoder angle that is already old when the resulting voltage reaches the motor. A control cycle is four ISR slots of K PWM periods each (K is 0x3001,13). Slot 1 starts the SPI encoder read, which is where the rotor is sampled. Slot 2 turns the angle into sin/cos. Slot 3 runs the Park transform and the current PIs. Slot 4 writes the new duty, which loads L periods later. The duty is then held for the whole 4K-period cycle, so on average it acts 2K periods after it loads.

That pipeline is (5K + L) PWM periods. 5.0.0 computes it from the PWM settings and reports it on 0x3013,10 in 0.1 µs. The encoder's own delay, ISR entry and gate-driver delay go in 0x3013,5, a signed trim in µs. The drive advances the output angle by the observer's speed times the total. Current feedback is transformed with the unadvanced angle at which it was sampled. On the P4-42 at 60 kHz the pipeline is 6 periods (100 µs), and the v1.7.0 trim, re-measured on the test drive, is −33 µs.

What an uncompensated angle does at speed

During the delay the rotor turns by ωe·t. On the P4-42 at 12,000 rpm (1,400 Hz electrical), 60 µs is 30° electrical. The voltage lands in a frame that lags the rotor, so part of the torque current becomes +id. That strengthens the field, raises the voltage the motor needs, and lowers torque per amp as speed rises. At high speed it can run away. In 4.3.3, 0x3013,5 was a Q8.8 factor on the last cycle's count change. The shipping P4-42 v1.3.3 CSV sets it to 0, so those drives had no latency compensation.

Exact commutation angle for any pole count

4.3.3 took the electrical angle modulo res/pole-pairs counts, rounded down. When the pole pairs do not divide the encoder resolution, the frame drifts in a sawtooth over each revolution. With 4096 counts, that is ±0.3° electrical at 7 pole pairs and ±2.6° at 10. 5.0.0 computes pole_pairs × θm mod res, exact to 1/pole_pairs of a count (4f4d1ce). 0x3013,8 reads 1 on firmware that does this.

How the trim is calibrated

The p4sysid latency step measures the residual delay from the motor's own back-EMF:

  • On STM32 it predicts the trim from the encoder's datasheet latency plus a bench-fit MCU offset (−37 µs), and starts there if the present trim is more than 15 µs away. K64 drives have no prediction yet.
  • It spins the rotor in profile torque, with current capped by 0x6073, to 1500 rpm or half the speed limit. Then it sets the torque to zero and lets the rotor coast, in each direction.
  • With both current references at zero, the PIs output exactly the back-EMF. The host streams the voltage request and position from the logger, and fits the back-EMF angle atan2(ud, uq) = e0 + terr·ωe. e0 is an electrical-zero error; terr is the timing error. The same coast gives Ke and Kt.
  • It moves 0x3013,5 by −terr and coasts again, for up to three coasts. If the error does not shrink, it restores the previous trim.
  • The step passes at |terr| ≤ 2 µs, under 1° electrical at top speed, or a quarter of the starting error. A trim more than 15 µs from the prediction is not written; the result is flagged suspect instead.

One-pole-pair motors are spun to the speed limit, and the fit uses only the upper half of the coast. In commission, the identification coast is the first measurement, and the two Kt values must agree within 3 %.

FIELD WEAKENINGField weakening and the velocity limit

A motor's back-EMF rises with speed. Without field weakening, the top speed is the natural base speed. There, the back-EMF plus the R·i and ωLqiq drops fills the bridge's voltage circle: Umax = Vbus/√3 per phase, less the PWM's sampling margin. Above that speed the current loop has no headroom and torque falls to zero. Field weakening adds negative d-axis current. Through ωLdid it cancels part of the back-EMF, which returns voltage headroom so torque continues above base speed. The cost is current that makes no torque, and a field that opposes the magnet. 4.3.3 has no field weakening: its d-axis reference is always 0.

How 5.0.0 does it

5.0.0 follows NXP's AMCLIB_FW structure, running at 1 kHz (3ab156f, 8ece1e3; always built since 57c013b). The error is the averaged q-axis voltage headroom minus the q-current tracking error. After a moving-average filter, a PI with anti-windup turns it into an angle γ between γmin and 0. Then id = Imax·sin γ, and iq is clamped to what remains of the current circle. NXP rotates the torque demand. 5.0.0 rotates the current limit instead, so id depends on γ alone. Otherwise a zero torque demand above base speed would drop id to zero and let the bridge rectify into the bus. In the simulation model, a torque-0 step from 13,800 rpm did that and reached 60 V.

γmin = −asin(Id,max/Imax). Id,max is the smaller of two limits:

  • Useful id (9a12c2b): ψω²Ld/(R² + (ωLd)²). Past this, more −id raises the voltage. On a high-resistance P4-16 it is tens of mA, and without the cap the regulator wasted current at the demag limit.
  • Demagnetisation limit: a quarter of ψ/Ld, at most a quarter of the working peak (0x3011,15). It derates linearly from full value 30 °C below the magnet rating (0x220A) to 2 % at 10 °C below, with a 300 mA floor. The temperature is the hotter of the motor thermistor and the amplifier. With no thermistor, the drive assumes the hot end of an 80 °C rating. −id more than 6 % of ipeak past the limit raises fault 0x2390.

0x3017,1 and ,2 report |id reference| and the derated limit, in per-mille of the rated current like Motor.id (a0faee6); 0x3016,6 shows the same limit as motion.c uses it. ,3 reports voltage use in %, and ,4 a modulator saturation flag. ,5–,7 override the PI gains and the filter (×1000; 0 = default).

Anti-windup at the voltage limit

With γ at its floor, the q loop runs out of voltage well below the speed loop's current limits. In early builds the velocity integrator kept winding. With an unreachable 16,000 rpm target at 41 V, that ended in a regenerative burst and a demagnetisation fault. Since 398e14b, while field weakening is at its floor and the q PI sits on its clamp, the velocity integrator may only unwind. The velocity ramp is also pulled back to the actual speed, and the software-limit status bit is set. A P4-42 then held 11,600–12,200 rpm against that target with no fault.

Why the velocity limit rose

Latency compensation and the exact commutation angle keep the voltage aligned with the rotor at speed, so current no longer leaks into +id. Corrected decoupling puts the right cross-coupling voltage on each axis. Field weakening then extends torque past the natural base speed, within the demagnetisation limit.

PWM AND CONTROL RATESSeparate PWM and control rates, and the rate ladder

In 4.3.3 the current loop ran once every four PWM periods, so the PWM rate also set the control rate. PWM was limited to 1–80 kHz, and the K64 could not sustain 80 kHz. In 5.0.0 the two rates are set separately, and PWM runs from 20 to 200 kHz on the STM32 and 16 to 200 kHz on the K64.

How the two rates are set

A control cycle is four case slots, and each slot spans K PWM periods. K is 0x3001,13 (1–4, 0 = automatic). The control rate is fpwm / (4K). It is reported read-only on 0x3001,14. The largest K the drive supports is on 0x3001,15 (4 on both MCUs). A slot may not run faster than 80 kHz, so the control rate tops out at 20 kHz. The firmware raises K when needed to keep the slot under that limit. An NV value outside the range is clamped at boot, and an SDO write outside it is refused. ab76850 3c6e36a

The recommended rate ladder

The p4sysid pwm step chooses both rates from the measured winding. PWM candidates run from 20 kHz to 200 kHz in 20 kHz steps; below 20 kHz the switching is audible. It picks the lowest rung whose current ripple is at most 0.35 × icont peak-to-peak, which is 10 % rms and about 1 % extra copper loss. Every cost of switching grows with frequency, so the rung is rejected if it breaks any of these upper bounds:

  • Voltage headroom of at least 60 % once the ADC sampling window is reserved. That window is a fixed time, so it takes a larger share of the period at higher rates, and the lost voltage comes straight off top speed.
  • Dead-time loss, 2 tdead fpwm, of at most 5 % of the bus.
  • One duty-cycle count may change the current by no more than 0.2 A.
  • An optional operator cap, --max-pwm-hz.

The control rate is then the slowest fpwm/(4K) that still meets the motor's floor. The floor is the larger of two values: 10 samples per electrical cycle at top speed, and 12.8 kHz, which gives a current-loop bandwidth of 200 Hz at fctrl/64. If no K meets the floor, the next allowed PWM rung up is used. The chosen rate is written and saved by default; --keep-pwm only reports it.

Why separate rates help with different windings

Between switching edges, the phase current ramps at a rate set by the bus voltage and the winding inductance. At the worst case of 50 % duty, the peak-to-peak ripple is

Δipp = Vbus / (4 · Lph · fpwm)

Here Lph is the per-phase d-axis inductance, which is half of the line-to-line Lt in 0x3011,6. Ld is used because it is the smaller of the two. This is the formula p4sysid uses (fits.pwm_ripple_a). It ignores resistance and back-EMF, so it is an upper bound. The table uses Lt and icont from the v1.6.0 and v2.6.0 CSVs, at the bus voltage each motor is rated for.

With the rates separate, it keeps cogging compensation, streaming and a 15 kHz loop, at the cost of a larger current ripple at 50 % duty.

In 4.3.3 every PWM rate came with a fixed control rate of fpwm/4. A low-inductance winding needed a high PWM rate to keep ripple down, which also forced a 20 kHz control loop and its CPU cost. A high-inductance winding could not drop to a lower rate. With the rates separate, each winding gets the lowest PWM rate that keeps its ripple heat small. Its control rate is chosen separately from its electrical speed and the current-loop bandwidth it needs. Ripple is mostly heat and acoustic noise; the current sample sits at the centre of the zero vector, where the triangle averages out. So a lower PWM rate on a high-inductance winding saves switching loss and voltage headroom without making the control worse, and a higher one on a low-inductance winding no longer means more control work. One firmware and one board cover a wider range of windings, and each one runs at a reasonable operating point.

Not every shipped rate came from the chooser. The P4-42's 60 kHz is the chooser's pick: the highest rung its ceilings allow, below the 120 kHz its ripple rule asks for. The P4-16 runs 74 kHz, set during its commissioning before the ladder existed and kept because it tuned and verified cleanly. That is off the ladder, and above the 40 kHz that the 5 % dead-time bound would allow at 420 ns. The P4-37 CSV carries the 80 kHz its drive ran when the CSV was exported. The bench P4-37 has since moved to 60 kHz PWM / 15 kHz control, the chooser's pick, to make CPU room for cogging compensation. Re-running the chooser on a motor gives the recommended rate. From 5.0.4 a K64 runs 74 kHz as 73 kHz and 80 kHz as 79 kHz (see 0x3001,1).

CURRENT SENSECurrent-sense accuracy

Why current-sense accuracy matters

The current loop holds the current it measures, not the current that flows. A phase offset becomes ripple at once per electrical cycle. An α/β gain mismatch becomes ripple at twice per cycle. A sample taken away from the period average gives a steady torque error that grows with speed.

What changed in 5.0.0

  • A fixed zero instead of a null at every enable (6d45046, replaced by c576a7a). Midway through this release the drive re-measured the bias at every enable: it held 50 % duty for 128 control cycles and averaged the last 96. On the P4-42 bench this turned out to do harm. Each null landed somewhere different, with the β zero spread 5–10 counts (about 1.1 mA per count) at rest and up to ±80 counts right after a stop, while the gearhead was still unwinding. Every count of error gave about 0.3 rpm of once-per-electrical-cycle velocity ripple, and that ripple changed size and phase each time the drive was enabled: 2–21 rpm at ±30 rpm. The final 5.0.0 firmware drops the null. The zero is the NV bias 0x3008,3 / 0x3009,3, calibrated once at 50 % duty with the bridge switching, plus the drive-on offset 0x3008,8 / 0x3009,8. On the test P4-42 the ripple is now 2–5 rpm, and its phase no longer changes between enables.
  • Centred sampling (STM32 a896b09, K64 d6dbbae). 4.3.3 sampled just after the widest PWM edge. The current ramps during the zero vector, so that sample was off the average by an amount that grows with speed. On an STM32 at 30 kHz this hid a braking current while iq read 0, and coasting raised the bus from 29 to 39 V. The sample is now centred on the zero vector. It moves later only when the widest edge plus its settling time would overlap the centre.
  • Trigger offset 0x3016,7 (472846e, ad43150). A test object, normally 0. It shifts the sample by signed trigger-clock ticks (PDB bus clocks on the K64, TIM1 clocks on the STM32), so a host can sweep the whole zero vector.
  • Ripple correction 0x3011,13 (b3150cc). Lhf is the inductance seen by the PWM ripple, in µH line-to-line. With Lhf and Rt set, the drive corrects each sample to the period average. In a P4-16 model at 4000 rpm the error fell from −25 to +1 mA at 20 kHz. Setting 0 turns it off. The v1.6.0 CSVs set 121 µH (P4-37), 503 µH (P4-16) and 0 (P4-42).
  • Load-proportional offset. New objects 0x3008,7 / 0x3009,7 (slope) and 0x3008,8 / 0x3009,8 (drive-on offset). On the P4-16 the slope is 0.15–0.22 mA per mA.
  • Gate (0f0356a). Closed loop is refused unless bias is 2048 ± 100 and gain factor is 4096 ± 200.

Calibration procedure (p4sysid)

  • settling runs measure_isense_settling. The drive holds a DC current open-loop with ripple correction off. The host sweeps 0x3016,7 in 200 ns steps at 0.15, 0.3, 0.5, 0.75 and 1.0 × icont (capped at 0.8 ipeak), in all six sectors from 30° to 330° electrical. It writes to MaxSettlingTime 0x3001,5 the shortest wait at which no reading is off by more than 2 % of the top current or the noise floor. A longer wait narrows the widest allowed pulse and costs voltage, so shorter is better. On a P4-42 at 80 kHz, 300 ns gave a worst error of 73–91 mA at a 0.766 voltage limit. 0 ns gave 292–350 mA, and 1.3 µs gave 26–30 mA at only 0.446.
  • isense repeats the sweep at the run PWM rate. It fits Lhf from the zero-vector slope, −R·i/Lhf, and writes it to 0x3011,13. If the zero vector is too short to fit, it drops to a lower PWM rate.
  • slope and fold fit the load-proportional offset and the drive-on baseline.

These are per-board calibrations. They are kept in NV, not in the CSV.

PROTECTIONOvervoltage, demagnetisation, i²t and watchdogs

A motor that brakes returns its kinetic energy to the DC bus. If the supply cannot sink that current and there is no brake resistor, the bus voltage rises. On the bench, a hard brake with a 1 A supply pumped the bus from 29 V to 53 V in about 40 ms. 5.0.0 has two independent overvoltage checks for this: a fast trip that acts in about a tenth of a millisecond, and the configurable slow limits that 4.3.3 already had, now with defaults.

Fast trip: the ADC analog watchdog

On the STM32 drive, the ADC that converts the bus voltage has a hardware analog watchdog. It compares every raw bus-voltage conversion against the threshold in 0x2384,10 (0.1 V units, default 560 = 56.0 V). No software filter sits in that path. The ADC interrupt counts conversions over the threshold. Three in a row turn the bridge off from inside the interrupt, and any conversion under the threshold restarts the count, so one switching spike cannot trip it. The bus is converted in two of every three control cycles, so the trip acts 100–150 µs after the crossing at 80 kHz PWM and 0.5–0.75 ms at 16 kHz. 13c797e, 3ef1b28

  • Action. The PWM outputs are driven low. All three low-side switches conduct, which shorts the windings, so no current flows back to the bus through the body diodes. On the bench a trip at 2000 rpm stopped the rotor in about 60 ms.
  • Latching. The trip latches, and re-enabling the bridge is refused until the drive has entered FAULT. It raises fault 0x3280 (error register bit 2, voltage) with an EMCY. A fault reset (controlword bit 7) re-arms it.
  • Setting it. A write takes effect at once. Values from 10.0 V up to the bus full scale are accepted. An erased, zero or out-of-range NV value loads 56.0 V.
  • Margin. The raw conversions carry PWM ripple. On a P4-42 the highest single conversion was 0.1 V over the filtered bus at idle and 0.5 V at ±5000 rpm. 0x3016,9 reports the highest raw conversion seen (write 0 to restart). Set the trip several volts above the highest working bus voltage.

K64 status. The K64 drives (P4-37, P4-16) accept 0x2384,10 but do not act on it. At 5.0.0 (6c37573) the K64 hardware layer's trip functions are still empty stubs. On K64 hardware, rely on the slow overvoltage limit and the supply's own protection.

Four protection changes in 5.0.0 guard the motor magnets, the windings and the drive itself. None of them needs configuration beyond the motor data already in the CSV.

Temperature-derated demagnetisation limit

Negative d-axis current weakens the rotor field. Field weakening uses it on purpose, and a motor driven past its voltage limit draws it whether or not it is commanded. Too much of it, on a hot magnet, demagnetises the rotor permanently. 4.3.3 had no limit on it.

  • The cold limit is derived from the motor data: 25 % of the characteristic current ψ/Ld (from Kt, pole count and Lt), and never more than 25 % of the working peak (0x3011,15).
  • The limit is derated with temperature. It is full up to 30 °C below the magnet rating 0x220A (default 80 °C) and falls linearly to 2 % at 10 °C below it. With a valid motor thermistor (0x3010,3) it uses the hotter of the motor and the amplifier. It never goes below 300 mA.
  • Field weakening cannot command more than this limit. If the measured −id still exceeds it by 6 % of the working peak for 500 ms, the drive stops with fault 0x2390.
  • Without a valid motor thermistor the magnet temperature is unknown. The drive then assumes the hot end: an 80 °C rating at the bottom of its derate. On such motors the field-weakening current is held near the 300 mA floor.

Commits 28e5108, 334285f, 0156862; the limit now feeds the AMCLIB_FW regulator in field_weakening.c.

i²t with a capped accumulator and one d/q budget

The i²t limiter folds the drive back toward its continuous current (0x3011,8) when the integrated heating exceeds what ipeak (0x3011,9) for its rated time (0x3011,10) allows. Over the amplifier temperature limit 0x2384,9 it folds to 0x3025,3. Three faults in 4.3.3 are fixed:

  • The accumulator was unbounded. After a long overload it held about 16 times the limit and took that long to recover, so the next run started already limited. It now stops at the limit, and 0x3025,1 reads 0–1000 (per mille of the limit). 6d45046
  • Heating is computed from the full current vector, √(id² + iq²). When the limiter acts, it shrinks the radius of the current circle in the current loop, so id and iq share one budget. Field-weakening current can no longer ride on top of a folded iq. 6d45046, 911d081
  • An off-by-one in the fixed-point conversion took one count off every unlimited command. A temperature limit 0x2384,9 of 0 now means off. bd9fc7a, a896b09

In IDLE the limiter is fed zero current, so a drifting current-sense zero cannot accumulate phantom heat while the bridge is off.

5.0.1 changes what the limiter counts. 5.0.0 fed it one filtered and clipped current sample per millisecond. In a velocity-loop oscillation that sample under-read the heating; on the test P4-42, in a 125 Hz bang-bang limit cycle, the limiter counted 0.93 of the true mean |I|². The control interrupt now sums id² + iq² from the unsaturated, unfiltered currents every control cycle, and the 1 kHz tick takes the r.m.s. of that sum; the same test counts 0.997–0.999. While the limiter is folding, the accumulator may now run to twice the limit, so current that stays above icont during the fold still delays the release; 0x3025,1 reads up to 2000. With the r.m.s. feedback but the accumulator still capped at the limit, a sustained limit cycle on the P4-42 ran at 6.00–6.09 A r.m.s. against an icont of 5.96 A; with the carry it runs at 5.97 A. 5319d2e, 37e7879, 9a20a85, 6297913

Working peak and the overcurrent trip

The current the drive will actually command is the working peak, 0x3011,15 (read-only, mA): the smallest of the motor's ipeak (0x3011,9), the amplifier's hard current limit 0x3001,8, and 95 % of the current-sense full scale on the α channel. 0x3011,16 is a bitmask of which layers bind: 1 = ipeak, 2 = amplifier, 4 = sense range. All current clamps and the demagnetisation limit are fractions of it. The hard overcurrent trip is now min(0.98 × full scale, 1.25 × working peak), tested on the raw d and q currents (0x3010,9 and 0x3010,12) before any filtering. In 4.3.3 the trip compared a scaled value that on one axis could never reach its threshold. dc5c63b

Watchdog on both MCUs

A firmware hang now resets the drive within 100 ms on both processors. The STM32 watchdog was nominally 1 s but actually 32.8 s. The K64 watchdog was configured but never enabled, so a hung K64 stayed silent until power was cycled. Both refresh from the main loop every 20 ms. Non-volatile saves, which block for up to 46 ms on the STM32 and 51 ms on the K64, refresh it so that a burst of saves cannot trip it. 0x3000,3 reports the reset source after a watchdog reset. b5f79a2, bc04a63, defd53e, 53536c2

On the STM32, a watchdog reset also stops the flashloader from relaunching the same application. The puck waits in the flashloader for a new image instead of looping through crashes.

Exact bus-voltage compensation

The drive scales its voltage command by nominal bus (0x3001,9) over live bus, so the current loop gain does not change as the supply sags. In 4.3.3 that product was formed in 32-bit fixed point and overflowed once the live bus fell below half the nominal value. A full positive command then came out as full negative voltage. On a 42 V nominal setup that starts at 21 V, which is above a typical undervoltage limit, so nothing tripped. Load sags the bus, the compensation raises the command, and the overflow feeds itself. 4ee599e

The first fix capped the scale at 2×. That made every applied voltage short below half nominal, which biased identification: on a P4-42 run from 19 V every voltage was 8 % short, so R and L read 8–12 % high. 5.0.0 runs the current loop in 32-bit (single-precision) floating point on the MCU's FPU. The scale is a float ratio, nominal over live bus, which cannot wrap, and only the result is limited to the voltage circle (GFLIB_VectorLimit), in the requested direction. Requests the bus can deliver are applied exactly at any bus voltage, to float precision (24-bit mantissa), far finer than the PWM's duty resolution. 3038ef4 a187580

The tested P4-37 and P4-16 motors have no thermistor. The drive then assumes the hot end of an 80 °C magnet rating, so their demagnetisation limit, and with it the field-weakening current, sits at the 300 mA floor. Fit a thermistor and set 0x3026 to use the full limit.

i²t counts d current too

The same current magnitude was applied twice: once on the q axis (torque mode with the encoder offset shifted so the rotor stays put) and once on the d axis (a DSTEP while the velocity loop holds 0 rpm). 4.3.3 integrated only iq², so d current, including field-weakening current, heated the motor without being counted. 5.0.0 integrates iq² + id² into one budget.

MOTOR BEHAVIOURMode switching, catching a spinning rotor, coasting

Bumpless mode switching

In 4.3.3 a mode switch restarted the outer loop from rest. Entering a velocity mode zeroed the target, the ramp and the integrator. Entering a torque mode zeroed the torque target. A spinning motor was braked. In 5.0.0, a switch between two closed-loop modes keeps the current loop and its PI state (911d081). The new mode starts from the running state (c7cf4e1). A velocity mode takes the present speed as target and command, and the present torque demand as integrator. A torque mode takes the present torque demand. From IDLE, and into or out of phase-voltage mode, the current loop still starts from zero.

Catching a spinning rotor at enable

IDLE is a resistive brake, and a load or inertia can keep the rotor turning. NXP's AMCLIB_Windmilling finds such a rotor's angle and speed from the phase voltages. This board does not sense phase voltages, but the encoder and the tracking observer know angle and speed in IDLE too. So 5.0.0 does only the hand-off (9859600). In a velocity mode entered from IDLE, the target stays 0, but the demand and ramp start at the measured speed, and the drive decelerates at 0x6084. The back-EMF feed-forward (54b0459) gives the q axis the right voltage from the first cycle, so the current loop does not brake against the back-EMF. In the simulation model, re-entering velocity mode after 30 ms of IDLE at 12,000 rpm used to pump the bus to 60 V; with the hand-off, the bus peaked at 42.7 V. On the K64 the observer now accounts for the slower ISR in IDLE (6a0e59a). Before that, an enable after a move could spin a P4-16 to 1,000–1,700 rpm.

5.0.4 closes the remaining K64 gap in this hand-off. The tracking observer rang at the slow IDLE rate of about 154 Hz, and from about 5100 rpm it could lock falsely at 1/2 or 1/3 of a revolution per sample for over 1.6 s, so profile velocity with target 0 could turn the rotor up to 15 revolutions. In IDLE the K64 now differences the angle for speed (f384051), and it stays at the full interrupt rate for 50 ms after entering IDLE (d93342b). On a K64 bench drive with a Moons motor stopping from about 5100 rpm, re-entering profile velocity after 5 ms of IDLE moved 111 counts (6,464 without the 50 ms hold) and after 10 ms moved 16 (7,838 without it). In cyclic synchronous velocity, the 0x606C reading 2 ms after IDLE peaked at 62,424 counts/s, against a false 647,045. The STM32 does not slow its interrupt in IDLE.

Zero torque coasts

A zero torque command regulates iq to 0 with the q PI still running. The back-EMF feed-forward carries ωψ, so the bridge does not brake. Above base speed, field weakening rotates the current limit rather than the demand. The −id is therefore held when the torque drops to zero, and the back-EMF never exceeds what the bridge can oppose. It decays as the motor slows and headroom returns, reaching zero at base speed. A separate hold-and-decay feed-forward did this during development (d3032ea). The field-weakening structure replaced it (3ab156f).

No closed-loop current on an uncalibrated current sense

An erased current-sense gain reads 0 A whatever flows. The current loop then winds to full voltage, blind to the over-current guard and i²t. On the bench this browned out the supply. Since 0f0356a, closed-loop modes and identification injections require both channels' bias within ±100 ADC counts of mid-scale and gain factor within ±200 of 4096 (about ±5 %). Otherwise the request is refused with an SDO abort and fault 0x6320. A running mode faults if the calibration leaves that window. IDLE and phase-voltage mode, where the calibrations run, are always allowed.

Firmware 4.3.3 + v1.3.3 CSVFirmware 5.0.0 + v1.7.0 CSV

In the mode-switch test the motor spun at 1000 rpm in velocity mode, switched to torque mode for 100 ms, and returned to velocity mode. 4.3.3 zeroed the velocity target on re-entry, reversed to −320 rpm and stopped. 5.0.0 picked up from the running state with no dip: the slowest sample after re-entry was 1,764 rpm, already above the 1000 rpm it left, on its way to the new target. A zero-torque command from 1000 rpm coasted to rest in 176 ms on 5.0.0 (177 ms on 4.3.3). At this speed the gearhead's friction sets the stop time, and the bus moved less than 0.5 V on either firmware. The coast and hold-and-decay matter at higher speeds, where braking would pump the bus. When the host's heartbeat stopped, 4.3.3 kept the motor running, since it has no heartbeat consumer. 5.0.0 faulted with 0x8130 and stopped. Its trace ends near the 300 ms consumer time, because a faulted 5.0.0 drive stops sending PDOs; the statusword read afterwards shows the fault.

CANOPENDS301 / DS402 compliance and robustness

5.0.0 was reviewed against CiA 301 v4.2 and CiA 402. The changes below affect how a drive behaves on a shared bus and how it reacts when the host or the bus fails. Commit hashes are in the stm32 repository.

Bus and network robustness

  • SYNC lockup fixed. On 4.3.2 and 4.3.3 fleets, a freshly powered puck could go silent within a few SYNC frames and stay silent while SYNC continued. The cause was a PDO mapping whose entry count was larger than its valid records: the firmware armed it without checking and jumped through a null pointer on the first SYNC. Mappings are now validated before they are armed, and a bad one is refused with SDO abort 0x06040041 or 0x06040042. Two related defects are also fixed: a PDO with a stored COB-ID of 0 transmitted on the NMT identifier and could stop or reset every node on the bus, and the statistics block read address 0 on every SYNC. 2168320, f32c67c, 127977b
  • Bus-off recovery. On bus-off the STM32 CAN controller stops driving the bus, and nothing restarted it, so the node stayed off the bus until a power cycle. The drive now discards its pending frames and rejoins after the mandatory 129 × 11 recessive bits. With 0x2405 set, it also raises fault 0x8140. The K64 CAN driver does not detect bus-off yet. 9a0ef3a, 627713d
  • SYNC no longer replays stale RPDOs. 4.3.3 applied every synchronous RPDO on every SYNC, whether or not a new frame had arrived, and ignored the COB-ID valid bit. A zero controlword was replayed and the drive left Operation Enabled. Only an RPDO received since the last SYNC is applied now. 911d081
  • SYNC is consumed in PRE-OPERATIONAL. CiA 301 makes SYNC valid in PRE-OPERATIONAL. Before this, a node that had not been started ignored SYNC, so the cyclic modes stayed in their sync-lost state. PDOs still need OPERATIONAL. 64bfe87

Biggest gaps left (review of 2026-11-05, for 5.0.5)

The earlier list is closed: statusword bit 9 is set, 0x6060 is accepted in any state, 0x607D = 0/0 turns the limits off, 0x1200 is read-only, unsupported PDO types are refused, and a bus-off changes the NMT state as 0x1029 says. What is left, most important first:

LOGGEROn-board data logger 0x3004 and build stamp 0x3003

5.0.0 adds an on-board logger at 0x3004. It samples up to 16 object-dictionary values in the current-control interrupt, at the control rate, which is far faster than any host can poll over CAN. 4.3.3 had no equivalent. ed71075, 0ea2320, dfb25c9

Choosing what to log

The channels are the objects mapped into TPDO4 (0x1A03), in mapping order. Any numeric object can be a channel, and each is read in its own data type: signed, unsigned or REAL32. The logger has 2 KB of memory. Two 16-bit channels make a 4-byte record, so 512 records; decimation (0x3004,3) stretches a capture over a longer time. Fast internal signals that used to need special capture sources are ordinary objects now: 0x3010,7–11 hold the alpha and beta currents, the unfiltered iq, the rotor-frame iq (mA) and the low 16 bits of position, all written every control cycle.

Three modes (0x3004,15, latched when armed)

  • 0, raw capture. One record per sample, laid out as TPDO4's payload. Use it for step responses and waveforms.
  • 1, statistics. The minimum, maximum, mean and standard deviation of each channel, plus the sample count, over a set number of samples or until stopped. Use it for ripple, noise and offsets over long runs, without moving the data.
  • 2, execution timing. The channels are code locations 0x302A,1–16 (the four PWM interrupt phases, the FOC, the ADC, CAN and 1 kHz tick interrupts, the motion loop, field weakening, the main loop and its sleep, one received CAN frame and one SYNC). Each is timed every time it runs, in ticks of 2n core cycles (0x3004,16; rate on 0x3004,17), and kept as statistics. Use it to check the CPU headroom at a chosen PWM rate.

Reading it out

Over SDO, set the cursor 0x3004,4 to a record (raw) or a statistic (0 min, 1 max, 2 mean, 3 stdev, 4 count) and read 0x3004,5 once per channel; reading the last channel advances the cursor. Over PDO, writing 3 to 0x3004,1 plays the data back through TPDO4 in its live layout, one record or statistic per transmission. On an STM32 drive with a CAN FD SYNC, TPDO4 carries up to 64 bytes, so 16 channels fit one frame.

Streaming over PDO

For captures longer than the buffer, 0x3004,9 starts an integrating stream. Every control cycle the drive adds iq (mA), ud and uq to running sums, counts the cycle and notes the position. At each SYNC the set is snapshotted together into 0x3004,10–14 for a TPDO. The host differences consecutive frames and gets the exact integral over an exact number of cycles, whatever the SYNC jitter, and a lost frame loses no data. p4sysid's drag test, which identifies inertia and friction, runs on this stream.

EXTRASPurr, multi-puck sync and SINE voices

Two features in 5.0.0 are not part of motion control. They came out of demonstration work and are documented here because their objects are in the EDS.

Purr and multi-puck synchronisation

0x302B is a purr generator: a small torque overlay, added to whatever mode the drive is in, that makes the axis feel and sound like a purring cat. Rate, tone, symmetry, variability, filter, volume and noise are sub-indices 2–8. A model of the motion the overlay causes (Kt·i/J, with a leak corner in 0x302B,10) is subtracted from the position and velocity loops' feedback, so the loops do not fight it; 0x6064 and 0x606C still report the real motion. The model needs J in 0x3011,7. None of these objects is stored in NV, so a drive never purrs after a reboot unless the host turns it on. fb66c7b

Several axes can purr as one. Everything random in the generator comes from one seeded pseudo-random sequence, so the same settings, the same seed (0x302B,13) and the same start tick give bit-identical overlays. A 0→1 write to 0x302B,1 restarts the generator, which makes it a synchronisation signal: map it into an RPDO on a COB-ID the group shares, and one frame starts every axis on the same SYNC. 62cb9a2

Shared start alone is not enough. The STM32's internal oscillator can differ by about 2 % between units, which walks a 1.7 s purr out of phase within a minute. On the STM32, the drive locks its clock to the SYNC producer using the CAN controller's receive timestamps, dithering between the two oscillator trim codes that bracket the producer's rate. The motion tick is a 1 kHz interrupt on that clock. The K64 does not support this clock lock. 62cb9a2

SINE "music" voices

The identification injection 0x3015 has a SINE mode (0x3015,1 = 3) that drives a sinusoidal voltage on one axis (0x3015,7: d or q) for a bounded time (0x3015,8). The winding current at audio frequency makes the motor an audible speaker. 5.0.0 sums up to three voices: voice 1 is the amplitude and frequency in 0x3015,5/6, voice 2 is 0x3015,11/12 and voice 3 is 0x3015,13/14. Amplitude is a fraction of the maximum voltage (Q15) and frequency is in Hz. Each voice keeps its own phase when the SINE is re-armed with new notes, so a host can play a melody note by note. Voices 2 and 3 are cleared when the injection stops. On the bench, a held D–F♯–A chord showed only 294, 370 and 440 Hz in the measured current. ca1198d

Keep the amplitude low. SINE is an open-loop voltage, so the current is set by the winding impedance at that frequency; the hard current guard, i²t and the duration cap still end it. puckutility's sandbox/play_song.py plays MIDI files this way.

UPGRADINGBreaking changes: read before flashing

Most of 5.0.x is backward compatible. The changes below are not. Items that name 5.0.0 hold on every later 5.0.x unless they say otherwise. Loading a 4.3.3 CSV onto 5.0.x gives wrong motor values without any error, because several objects kept their index but changed their unit. Before upgrading, export or record the drive's settings. After flashing, load one of the CSVs listed below through the p4core loader, or re-run commissioning.

ConvertPer-mille is of the rated current, and the gains rescale with it

In 4.3.3, a torque or current per-mille (Motor.id, CurrentFeedback, 0x6071, 0x6077) was per-mille of ipeak, capped at ±1000. In 5.0.0 it is per-mille of the rated current icont. 0x6075 reads icont as an rms value, 0x6076 is the rated torque Kt × icont, and demands above 1000 are allowed up to the working peak. On the test P4-42 1000 ‰ was 28.3 A and is now 5.96 A. Writing 0x6076 sets Kt. The velocity and impedance gains output torque in these units. On the first boot the drive therefore rescales 0x2381,1–4, 0x2383,1–2 and 0x6087 once, and sets the marker 0x2386,5. The p4core CSV loader scales the same rows of an older CSV by the CSV's rated torque over Kt × icont / √2 and skips its 0x6076 row. Any other tool writes those gains unscaled. They are then several times off, with no error, so load old CSVs only through p4core. dc5c63b ecdc33c cf8d11e

CheckSTM32 PWM is 20–200 kHz

On STM32 the PWM frequency 0x3001,1 must be 20–200 kHz in whole kHz. An SDO write outside that range is refused. A value already in NV is clamped into range at boot. On 4.5.0 development builds, a 16 kHz setting wrapped the 16-bit dithered period. The watchdog then reset the drive and it ended up in the flashloader. The K64 floor stays at 16 kHz. aabf97d

CheckPDO mapping lengths must match the object

A mapping record whose bit length does not match the mapped object's size is now refused with SDO abort 0x06040041. A mis-sized mapping saved in NV is refused at boot, so the PDO comes up unmapped. The legacy P4-Checkout P4-32-BO3518.csv maps 0x3025,1 as 32 bits (0x30250120). That row must be fixed before the CSV loads on 5.0.0. fc554c0

Re-commissionRt and Lt change units with no migration

0x3011,5 (Rt) changes from 0.01 Ω to mΩ, and 0x3011,6 (Lt, now Ld) from 0.01 mH to µH. Both are line-to-line, which is twice the per-phase value. Lq is new at 0x3011,12, also in µH line-to-line. Neither the firmware nor the CSV loader converts Rt or Lt, so a value already in NV or loaded from an old CSV reads 10× too low. That mis-sizes the current-display filter, decoupling and dead-time compensation. The P4-42 rows show the scale: Rt 28 becomes 290 and Lt 17 becomes 98.

CheckEncoder latency 0x3013,5 is now a trim in microseconds

In 4.3.3, 0x3013,5 was a lag factor that defaulted to 0. In 5.0.0 the firmware computes the pipeline delay itself and shows it on 0x3013,10 in 0.1 µs. 0x3013,5 is now a signed trim in µs; the P4-42 default is −34. The value is not migrated, and the CSV row still carries the old "Encoder lag factor" label. An old CSV's 0 overrides the default, so take the value from commissioning. 0x3013,7 is obsolete: it is ignored, but still range-checked so old CSVs load.

ChangeMotor polarity moves to 0x607E

0x3013,3 is removed. Polarity is now DS402 0x607E: 0x00 for normal and 0xC0 for inverted. Until 0x607E has been saved, the drive reads the old setting from its retired NV slot at every boot, so the motor keeps its direction; the next Save.All stores it in 0x607E. The p4core loader rewrites an old 0x3013,3 row to 0x607E. Other CANopen tools get an SDO abort on 0x3013,3. 8a366c6

ChangeKt moves to REAL32 0x3011,14

Kt is now a float at 0x3011,14 in mNm/A rms. On first boot the drive takes the old whole-number Kt from its retired NV slot until ,14 is saved. 0x3011,4 stays as a rounded mirror, and a write to it also sets ,14. c330a51

CheckCurrent-sense zero is calibrated, not re-nulled

5.0.0 takes the current zero from NV: the bias 0x3008,3 / 0x3009,3 (calibrated at 50 % duty with the bridge switching) plus the drive-on offset 0x3008,8 / 0x3009,8. It does not re-measure the zero at enable. A wrong bias keeps its error, so run the bias calibration as part of commissioning and after flashing. Even then, the zero is not fixed. On the test P4-42 the β zero varied by up to 5.6 counts between boots (2050.5–2056.1 over seven boots on two days), and a boot 4 counts from the stored bias made the 5 rpm position wobble 16° instead of 7°. Calibrate the bias as the mean of several boots, not from one. 5.0.1 removes the main cause on STM32 drives (next item). c576a7a

Re-calibrate5.0.1 on STM32: run the bias calibration once

On the P4-42 the β channel’s zero is a fixed 1.65 V read against the ADC reference, which drifts by a few millivolts, so the zero moved about 0.6 counts per mV. From 5.0.1 the STM32 drives scale β by the internal reference: 20 s means at rest varied 1.25 counts peak to peak before, 0.28–0.36 after. The reference to scale against, 0x3009,9 (new NV slot 320), is recorded by the bias calibration; while it is 0 or erased, the correction is off and the drive behaves as 5.0.0. 5.0.1 also sets the STM32 ADC sampling time from 0x3001,6 instead of a fixed 12.5 cycles (the P4-42 template now uses 613 ns), and changing 0x3001,6 moves the zeros by about 9.5 counts. So after flashing 5.0.1, or changing 0x3001,6, re-run the bias calibration and save. K64 drives have no such drift and are unaffected. 189b1c1

Check5.0.2 on STM32: encoder latency shifts about 1 µs

5.0.2 shortens the encoder read in the PWM interrupt, so the encoder samples its angle about 1 µs earlier. That is about 0.04 electrical degrees at 1000 rpm with 7 pole pairs. Re-run the latency step at the next commissioning; it need not be redone just for the upgrade. bd89f7e

ChangeCurrent-sense bias in Q12.4

0x3008,3 and 0x3009,3 change from ADC counts to Q12.4 (counts × 16). The drive converts an old value from NV at boot and keeps it in RAM; the next save writes the new format. A value written over SDO is not converted, so a tool that writes a bias of about 2048 counts must write about 32768. Re-running the bias calibration is simplest. a896b09

ChangeCogging bins in 0.1 mA

The 0x302E amplitude bins change from mA to 0.1 mA in the same NV slots. A new read-only marker, 0x302E,16, records the unit. When it reads erased or 0, the drive scales the saved table by 10 at boot (with saturation) and marks it. A cogging table written to the drive as whole mA by a script would apply one tenth of the intended current. ba0c0c7

ChangeCSP velocity feed-forward uses the commanded velocity

In cyclic synchronous position mode, 4.3.3 scaled the velocity feed-forward (0x2382,2) by the measured velocity 0x606C. That is positive feedback, and it cancels the loop's damping. In 5.0.0 it defaults to the commanded velocity, the interpolation slope per SYNC period. Profile position already used the profile velocity. 0x2382,5 (RAM only) chooses the source: 0 or 2 for commanded, 1 for measured. A host that relied on a nonzero 0x2382,2 in CSP will see different following error. On a P4-16 the maximum following error fell from 31° to 4.5°. 87bdfa4 e544a22

Change0x100A software version is now a string

4.3.3 reports a packed U32 (0x04000303). 5.0.0 reports the VISIBLE_STRING "5.0.0". The build's git hash and a dirty flag are in 0x3003.

ChangeOvervoltage and undervoltage timing

In 4.3.3 the slow faults ran only when their timeout (0x2384,4/5) was set. In 5.0.0 an unset value falls back to 52.0 V over 50 ms for OV and 18.0 V over 50 ms for UV. A CSV that sets the timeout to 500 ms, as the current templates do, keeps the 500 ms delay. On STM32, the fast trip 0x2384,10 (default 56.0 V) covers the gap.

ChangeSmaller changes

0x3011,2 accepts only ±1. The velocity filter corners 0x2100,2–3 are refused above 450 Hz, the Nyquist limit of the 1 kHz loop; a 1000 Hz setting had put the filter pole on the sample rate. A write to 0x2100,1–3 now takes effect at the next tick in IDLE instead of after a save and reset. Position Kp 0x2382,1 is the loop bandwidth in rad/s. The host tools read 0x3024,4 as the velocity bandwidth in 0.1 Hz (0 = automatic), not a gain factor, so the old CSV's 100 becomes a 10 Hz design target. The 4.3.3 tuning objects for on-drive R/L measurement are gone; identification now runs on the host through the 0x3015 injection object. The 100 ms watchdog replaces the 32.8 s STM32 watchdog; the K64 had none. 0x2110–0x2112 are removed, so a CSV row for them gets an SDO abort. 0x3011,17–18 (friction Tc and B) are new, stored for the host tools only.

The checklist runs some steps later than their stage allows:

  • The continuous-current (thermal) test requires only R (stage 4) but runs last. It is the longest test, and it sets the rated torque from Kt. On 5.0.0 every torque per-mille is a per-mille of icont. When icont changes, the step rescales every torque-unit gain so the torque it commands stays the same: velocity kp/ki, the Coulomb feed-forward 0x2381,3, settle ki 0x2381,4, the torque slope 0x6087, and any DS402 torque limit that is set (0x6072, 0x6073, 0x60E0, 0x60E1; 65535 means unset and is left alone). The cogging bins are in 0.1 mA, not per-mille, so they are not rescaled.
  • The current-loop step test (stage 8) runs after the stage-9 steps, because it uses the drag's J when it has one.
  • The PWM step saves the chosen rate and reboots the drive. 5.0.0 applies the rate and the pole count in IDLE, but 4.3.x latches them at boot, so the step reboots on every firmware. The board steps measured at the old rate (sample timing, slope, offset fold) then run again straight after it. The current-sense window step comes after PWM in the order anyway, so it runs once, at the new rate.

Two tune steps were added to the checklist for this release:

  • Coulomb friction feed-forward (0x2381,3). This adds ±Tc, signed by the trajectory velocity and zero at rest. It starts from 80 % of the drag step's Tc and runs PP moves with the feed-forward off and on. The feed-forward is kept only if the rms following error drops by at least 10 % and the overshoot grows by no more than 1.5 % of the move.
  • Settle ki (0x2381,4). This is an integrator that acts only at rest, inside 16 counts of the target. The step tries a ladder of multiples of the velocity ki. It rejects any rung that leaves a move unsettled or makes the hold noisier. It keeps the smallest rung within 25 % of the best settle time, and only if that rung beats settle ki off by 25 %. On node 4 the best value moved with temperature, so check it warm and cold before saving.

The board calibration is one shared sequence. pucktuner and p4gui run p4sysid.boardcal: Calibrate Active Puck in pucktuner, Calibrate and Auto-Configure in p4gui, and the calibrate command in each CLI. puckutility's Actuator Calibration menu and calibrate command run the same steps from p4core.actuator.plans, then the encoder runout table and cogging. The sequence runs encoder test, bias, gain, slope, offset fold, then encoder zero, in stage order. The fold now runs before the encoder zero, so the zero's d-axis sweep runs on corrected current. Steps named on the command line are sorted into stage order. p4gui's Calibrate now includes the slope and fold, which it used to skip. Its Identify list follows the stages too: PWM comes after the encoder offset, and the continuous current is last. The sample-timing step is left out of the whole sequence and runs only on its own. The checklist's settling step needs the motor's R and saves its own result.

Together these cover R, Ld/Lq, Kt, inertia J, viscous and Coulomb friction, encoder runout and latency, cogging, and the torque, velocity and position loop gains. python -m p4sysid commission runs the core of the same sequence without stopping, from a blank motor section to a saved configuration. It stops at the first failed stage. The operator guide is p4sysid/RUNBOOK.md. The GUIs call the same library functions: p4gui's Identify tab and the calibration menus in puckutility and pucktuner are views on p4sysid and p4core.

Check the step response after an automatic tune before shipping its gains. The P4-16 CSV's velocity and position gains were set by hand after its automatic tune.
